Water for today

About 7 bottles (~3.6 L) on top of the buttermilk, rasam and curd you're already getting at the mess — a bit above the usual 2.5–3 L baseline since you're eating/training on a calorie deficit. Water itself doesn't "burn fat," but staying well-hydrated keeps digestion (including breaking down the protein in your dal, curd and paneer) and metabolism running normally, and drinking a glass before meals is a real, evidence-backed way to curb overeating rice or fried snacks. Tap a bottle as you finish it.

Running rules

  • One fried item a day, max — cutlet, samosa, poori, bonda or vada, pick one, not the full count offered.
  • Rice: one small serving (⅓–½ cup) per meal — let phulka/roti carry the rest of the carbs.
  • Refill dal, sambar, rasam, curd and buttermilk freely — they're doing most of the protein and volume work here.
  • Sweets and milkshakes: skip on most days, half portion on the days you really want it.
  • Oats + peanut butter is fixed daily — 40 g Pintola oats with water, stirred with 1 tbsp (15 g) MyFitness peanut butter.
  • Drink water before each meal — it curbs how much rice or fried food you reach for.
  • Spread the ~3.6 L across the day rather than chugging it at once — your body absorbs it better, and steady hydration is what actually helps digest and use the protein in the dal/curd/paneer, not water in isolation.

About this app

A personal project I built to keep my hostel-mess diet on track: a two-week meal ledger with calorie and protein totals for each day, plus a daily water tracker. It runs fully offline and saves everything on my phone.

Built by VK · Version 1.1

Calories and protein are estimates from standard Indian-food references, not lab values — actual numbers shift with oil, ghee and portioning at your mess. Each day already carries a small buffer under 2,000 kcal; if you're still hungry, add rice or roti in ½-unit steps rather than an extra fried or sweet item.
